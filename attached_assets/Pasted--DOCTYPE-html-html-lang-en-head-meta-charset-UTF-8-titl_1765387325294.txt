    <section id="about">
      <h2>Why This Dashboard?</h2>
      <p>
        India’s drinking water and sanitation systems are increasingly exposed
        to climate extremes – heatwaves, floods, droughts, intense rainfall and
        cyclones. These events disrupt water supply, damage infrastructure and
        weaken service delivery for households, schools and health facilities.
      </p>
      <p>
        ClimateAdapt India is a decision-support MVP that converts the
        UNICEF–CEEW WASH climate risk assessment into a visual, district-level
        dashboard. It is designed for government departments, UNICEF and other
        development partners, and NGOs working on climate-resilient WASH.
      </p>
    </section>

    <!-- METHODOLOGY -->
    <section id="methodology">
      <h2>Methodology – Aligned with UNICEF–CEEW Study</h2>
      <p>
        This MVP follows the approach used in the UNICEF–CEEW study on climate
        risks to WASH systems in India.
      </p>
      <ul>
        <li>
          <strong>Framework:</strong> IPCC AR5 risk framing –
          <em>Risk = f(Hazard, Exposure, Vulnerability)</em>.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Indicators:</strong> A set of indicators representing climate
          extremes, WASH coverage and infrastructure, and socio-economic
          conditions, shortlisted through expert consultation.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Index Creation:</strong> Indicators are normalised, weighted
          and combined into a composite district-level risk score, then grouped
          into categories (Low, Medium, High, Very High).
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Geographic Scale:</strong> District-level analysis for all
          districts in India, with potential to extend to block/ULB level in
          future phases.
        </li>
      </ul>
      <p class="muted">
        Detailed indicator lists, weights and data sources will be refined and
        documented with WASH and climate experts as this prototype evolves.
      </p>
    </section>
